Why it matters

Students are entering an AI-saturated world faster than we are preparing them for it.

Generative AI now sits inside how undergraduates study, write, search, and plan their careers. Many students feel pressure to use it, unease about what it means for their future, and uncertainty about where healthy use ends. Most campuses respond with policy or tool training. Few help students with the inner side of the shift.

Anxiety

Worry about careers, competence, and keeping up can crowd out learning and wellbeing.

Competency

Students need practical judgment about when AI helps their thinking and when it replaces it.

Attention

Always-on tools fragment focus. Mindfulness gives students a trainable way back.

Ethics

Honest, reflective use of AI is a habit students can practice, not only a rule they follow.

The program

What AIM-YA is

A structured, four-week, research-based curriculum that weaves short guided practices together with reflection and discussion on how AI is shaping students' learning, work, and sense of self.

Practice

Contemplative foundations

Brief, accessible mindfulness practices that build attention and steadiness. No prior experience needed for students or facilitators.

Reflect

Critical AI engagement

Guided reflection on real AI use: what it offers, what it costs, and how to stay the author of your own thinking.

Apply

Companion app

A companion app, developed with a UCF computer science student team, carries practice and reflection between sessions.

Built to be studied, with validated measures

MAAS

Mindful Attention Awareness Scale: everyday present-moment attention.

PSS-10

Perceived Stress Scale: how unpredictable and overloaded life feels.

AIMS

AI-related outcomes measured alongside mindfulness and stress.

Ways to run it

Three tested ways to bring AIM-YA to campus

Each model comes from a live pilot. Choose the one that fits your students, or let us help you adapt one.

Model from UCF

Embedded in a course

Run inside an existing class such as yoga, mindfulness, or a discipline course. UCF is expanding from a yoga course to four sections and a new pilot with chemistry students.

Model from Wesleyan College

Peer-led small groups

Trained group leaders facilitate AI-mindfulness reflections with small groups of STEM undergraduates.

In development

First-year experience

Students who complete AIM-YA serve as ambassadors who lead it with incoming first-year students, gaining leadership experience.

Partnership

What a pilot partnership looks like

You receive

  • The full AIM-YA curriculum, including a condensed four-week version
  • Four facilitator training videos and an onboarding guide
  • Access to the companion app as it rolls out
  • Ready-made survey instruments and study protocol
  • A summary of findings from your site
  • Opportunities to co-present and co-author

You provide

  • A course, section, or student group for about four weeks
  • A facilitator: you, a colleague, a TA, or trained student leaders
  • Help coordinating with your IRB, if you join the research arm
  • A signed memorandum of understanding (MOU)
  • Brief feedback so the program keeps improving

Questions

Frequently asked

Don't see your question? Write to Dr. Haberlin directly.

Do facilitators need mindfulness experience?

No. The facilitator training videos and guide walk you through every session. Experienced practitioners can go deeper, and newcomers have everything they need to lead it well.

Does my institution have to join the research study?

No. You can run AIM-YA as a program only. If you want to contribute data, we will help you coordinate IRB review or a reliance agreement with UCF.

What does it cost?

Pilot partners receive the curriculum, training videos, and study materials at no cost. In return we ask for a signed MOU and feedback on how it went.

How much class time does it take?

The core program runs over about four weeks. We will work with you to fit sessions into your course schedule or group meetings.

Can it work outside psychology or wellness courses?

Yes. Current pilots include yoga and mindfulness courses, STEM students at Wesleyan College, and chemistry students at UCF. AI touches every discipline.

How is student data protected?

Research participation follows IRB-approved procedures, with informed consent and confidential handling of survey data. Students can take part in the program without joining the study.
